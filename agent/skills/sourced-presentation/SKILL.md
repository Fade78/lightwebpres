# Sourced presentation: evidence and guidance

A presentation joins a **deck of cards** to a **fully referenced long-form
article**. Here, a **page** means that complete presentation unit, whatever
its file format. A card is a step in the page's argument; a page can be a
step in an editorial series.

**The article is the source of the cards, never the reverse.** It establishes
the claims and relationships that the short form will carry. A card with no
backing in the article is an assertion with no file behind it.

Two forms serve different entries. A **reversal** confronts a widespread
belief with what is established. An **exposition** supplies pieces the reader
has never had. Do not manufacture a belief or a controversy for an exposition.

**The series supplies the local contract; this skill supplies the method.**
Audience, language, register, format, lengths, numbering, safety requirements and tool
settings belong to the specification. Follow explicit local rules; otherwise
use the defaults here. A formatting choice never licenses a false claim.

**Verification is asymmetric.** Many confirmed facts do not offset one
unsupported quotation. A check reported as completed is itself a claim:
name what was checked, at what source level, and what remains unverified.

## Locate the work before continuing

**Read the current state from disk, not recollection.** Consult the mandate,
specification, fact file, article and cards. Distinguish current decisions
from historical reports. A past claim of completion does not validate a
later revision or the rest of the series.

**A first draft and a correction are different jobs.** A new page follows
the chain below. An existing page resumes from what changed and what that
change invalidates. Reuse established, dated evidence; reopen affected,
ambiguous or time-sensitive claims rather than repeating every search.

**A discussion request is not a production request.** If the author asks to
discuss wording before rebuilding, propose the passage and its intended effect
first. Do not modify files, generate another presentation or install a proposed
skill revision as a substitute for that discussion.

## Guide at all three scales

**Reading order is optional for the reader; placement is deliberate for the
author.** Independence is not isolation. Supply enough context for someone
entering at a shared card or page, while making each unit advance the larger
argument for someone following the route.

Before drafting, outline the route from the largest relevant unit inward.
For each unit, record its **entry, gain and next opening**:

| Scale | Entry | Gain | Next opening |
|---|---|---|---|
| Series, when present | What brings this audience to the subject? | What understanding should the whole route build? | What informed choices or questions become possible? |
| Page | What question does this page take up, and what context must it supply? | What distinct contribution does it make to the series? | What consequence or question gives another page its purpose? |
| Card | What is the reader trying to understand here? | What single supported point should remain? | What inference, contrast or question does this prepare? |

**The parent argument is not evidence for its children.** The route is
provisional until the facts support it. A finding may change the page's
point and the series plan; do not strengthen a claim merely to preserve
the progression originally imagined.

**A transition expresses a relationship, not just a direction.** Show why
one point leads to a mechanism, consequence, comparison or choice. Do not
add a teaser to every card. The conclusion still completes its own point;
it must not depend on the next unit to become true or intelligible.

**Name destinations by subject and purpose, not position.** A useful link
says what another page explains, not merely "previous" or "next". Restate
the premise needed locally; send the reader elsewhere for depth, not for
the missing subject of this sentence.

**Preserve contributions, not only names or files.** Before merging,
moving or removing a unit, identify where its useful explanation will live.
A surviving brand name or bibliography entry does not preserve an argument
that readers can no longer encounter.

## The first pass: the chain

1. **Define the target and route.** Name the audience, its question or belief,
   the intended gain, and the page's place in the larger argument.
2. **Conduct or commission adversarial research.** Specify axes, the strongest findings
   and qualifications, source identities, statuses and exclusions.
3. **Read the actor's own sources**, when examining an actor's conduct or claims,
   including documents for other audiences and pertinent responses.
4. **Build the fact file.** Give each candidate claim a dated verdict, scope
   and precise source. Record rejections and unresolved points too.
5. **Write the article.** Establish its argument and the evidence for every
   point the cards may carry; develop what the short form cannot explain.
6. **Verify after writing.** Check written claims against their sources and
   make a source-led pass for important omissions.
7. **Design and write the cards from the article.** Prepare the complete
   takeaway, the reader's stake and likely reply before drafting the scene.
8. **Re-read, cross-check and hand off.** Test the reading paths and the
   card-page-series progression, then verify the actual delivered artifact.

Record decisions before advancing: the next step must inherit a file, not
an undocumented intention. Verification of the article and cross-checking
the cards are separate gates; neither replaces the other.

**The origin of a belief determines where to look.** For an actor's claim,
start from its documents. For an intuition induced by design, examine the
mechanism. For a claim kept alive by repetition, trace the stale source.
For an ordinary intuition, seek research that can qualify it.

**Research may confirm the belief.** Then explain what it gets right before
what it misses. If the evidence offers no reversal, choose an exposition or
another useful question. Do not invent a counter-case to keep the opening
more dramatic than the findings.

## What a change invalidates

An existing page does not restart the whole chain. Identify the change and
perform the checks it reopens:

| Change | Checks reopened |
|---|---|
| A factual claim or its scope | Its source, article passage, and every card carrying it |
| A card's wording | The complete card, its reading paths, and its agreement with the article |
| A quotation or translation | The original wording, context and status; other uses of the same source |
| Emphasis, caption or foregrounded figure | The scanning-only reading and the claim it now makes |
| A correction | The same class of defect across the relevant corpus |
| A unit inserted, removed or moved | Contributions, entry-gain-next relationships, counts, links and ordering claims |
| A section renamed or renumbered | References and anchors in both short and long forms |
| A specification rule | Earlier units affected by that rule, not just future writing |
| A tool or format change | What it renders, ignores or defaults differently; the actual output |

**A correction can create a defect.** Re-read the result against the half
that did not change. Removing repetition can install a contradiction;
shortening a quotation can remove the modality that made it accurate.
The disappearance of the old wording proves neither repair nor completeness.

**Record the remaining perimeter.** A corrected pilot does not make every
older page compliant. Locate related defects and state which units remain
to be handled; do not silently expand the authorized work or claim that
unread parts of the corpus passed.

## The file of facts

**No factual claim enters without a dated verdict:** confirmed, qualified,
alleged or rejected. Record individual claims separately even when several
support one card. Keep their source, population, context, measurement and
limits; record any relationship or derivation the presentation will assert.

**A commissioned synthesis provides leads, not proof.** Recheck identities,
dates, designs, figures and statuses at the sources. Then revisit the requested
axes: checking every returned figure cannot reveal an important question
that the synthesis never addressed.

**A real reference can be wrong for the sentence.** Verify what the document
actually establishes, not merely that its title or institution exists.
Read the restrictions as well as the finding. A source about one population,
condition or outcome cannot silently stand for another.

**Disclose ambiguity in the source note.** If a source's identity or relevant
wording remains unresolved, say so where it is cited, not only in the fact
file. A plausible reference is not a verified one; replace or omit it
when it cannot support the claim.

**A report of a source is not that source.** Follow the chain to the original
where possible. If access stops at an abstract, excerpt or later account,
record that boundary and cite the material actually supporting the claim.
Do not present an inaccessible body as fully checked.

**For a study, read the method and results, not the abstract alone.** An
abstract can state an association that the body restricts to one condition.
If the body cannot be obtained, retain only what the accessible evidence
supports and record what remains unresolved.

**Check replication for every load-bearing finding.** Repetition is not
replication merely because the studies share a topic. Compare tasks,
populations and outcomes; a different or failed result can bound the claim
rather than erase it. Preserve that distinction in the article.

**Record funding and relevant ties.** A conflict does not automatically
invalidate peer-reviewed work, but it affects its weight and must not be
hidden. Failure to find a disclosure is not a confirmed absence of conflicts.

**Trace attributed quotations to an identifiable original.** Interviews,
talks and texts carrying striking figures need particular care. If a phrase
circulates without a first utterance, do not attribute it by repetition;
use what the person is documented as saying.

**A translated quotation is marked and checked against its original.**
Preserve qualifications and context when excerpting. Respect applicable reuse
limits; moving a long quotation into a note does not remove those limits.
Paraphrase accurately when the necessary quotation cannot be reproduced.

### Weigh sources for the claim at hand

**Peer-reviewed research is the default for general findings.** An admission,
record or adjudicated decision may be stronger for a particular event.
Document type alone does not settle the comparison: examine what the source
can establish about this claim.

**Consider what the statement cost its author.** An admission against
interest can be unusually strong evidence of what was admitted. A document
addressed to investors, advertisers or recruits can reveal what another
audience had to be told. Neither proves more than its actual scope.

**Adjudicated facts are not allegations.** Keep the distinction between
a filing, a finding, a settlement and an admission. Likewise, an actor's
measurement or promise remains attributed unless independently established;
the actor's confidence is not independent verification.

**A self-serving statement needs the same scrutiny whoever makes it.**
That includes a defendant, a vendor and a campaign supporting the desired
conclusion. Do not waive the check because the source agrees with the
presentation's thesis.

**Ranking never licenses silent omission.** Preserve a pertinent weaker
source with its status. Between similarly situated sources, prefer the
more specific and better dated. If an unresolved disagreement changes the
claim, report it rather than choosing the convenient figure.

### Specify the research brief

Write the brief before searching. It includes:

- Named axes, one for each substantive question.
- The strongest finding and counter-case on each axis, with the relevant
  positions named. An exposition needs qualifications, not manufactured camps.
- Authors, year, venue, design, population, finding and publication status
  for each study or document, so it can be checked again.
- What is not established, explicitly; absence of measurement is an acceptable answer.
- Exclusions against overlap with other pages, checked so they do not remove
  a neighboring question that is covered nowhere.

**Seek the counter-case where it challenges the argument most.** Do not
source one side from research and the other only from publicity, or stop
when a synthesis agrees. Examine the best pertinent evidence rather than
giving each side an equal amount of space.

### Keep quantities meaningful

**A quantity carries its unit of observation.** Distinguish per-person time
from study duration, continuous stretches from cumulative totals, and group
averages from individual bounds. Name the population, comparison and time
window needed to understand the number.

**Name the thing measured, not a larger construct.** A delay to the next
action is not recovery of full concentration; time before returning to a
task can include other activities. Neither measures how long a person was
unable to concentrate.

**A proportion needs a matching numerator and denominator.** Two durations
do not establish the claimed share merely by having the same unit. Record
the basis of a calculation in the article or fact file; do not invent a
percentage to make a conclusion more striking.

**Match precision to the evidence.** Preserve meaningful exact counts and
thresholds. Use an identified order of magnitude for uncertain estimates;
do not decorate a broad range with decimals. When sources differ, justify
any retained approximation and leave the unresolved difference visible.

## Read the actor's sources and responses

This section applies when examining an actor's conduct or claims, not merely
because a researcher is named in a citation.

**Look beyond material addressed to the reader.** Documents for advertisers,
investors or other customers can reveal what is sold, collected or promised.
Verify a contradiction between documents in its dates and scopes; different
audiences alone do not establish that the statements conflict.

**The actor's words cut both ways.** A favorable claim is verified where
possible and attributed where not. A pertinent response can narrow or correct
the finding. Do not omit it, adopt a general defense as a verdict, or invent
a rebuttal to preserve an earlier accusation.

**A response is evidence of the response, not automatic proof of its content.**
Read what it concedes, contests and leaves unanswered. An unanswered point
is not an admission: it still needs its own evidence. The selection may
matter, but silence cannot supply the missing fact.

**Do not stop at the reply.** Seek relevant answers to the response as a
separate step. Leaving the last word with whichever party was found last
is an accident of search order, not an assessment of the evidence.

**For a new allegation formulated by the article, seek the actor's response**
within the authorized reporting workflow. Reporting existing research does
not require manufacturing an exchange, but pertinent published responses
must not disappear. Unapproved outreach is not implied by a writing request.

**Read guarantees to their exact scope.** Not selling data is not the same
as not holding it; protection against one actor is not protection against
every actor. State the relevant boundary rather than negating all protection
or presenting a limited safeguard as a general one.

**Archive dated wording, not only its address.** Mutable pages can change
after a claim is checked. Do not call public material secret or leaked
because the intended reader rarely sees it; its audience can explain its
importance without inventing concealment.

## Establish the article

**The article has its own argument and sufficient local context.** It answers
the page's question while contributing to the series when one exists.
Develop every claim and relationship the cards will carry, and the methods,
limits and additional evidence that make their short statements defensible.

**Its scope exceeds the deck's.** An article that merely expands the cards
gives them little to stand on. Preserve useful findings not selected for
the short route, without turning the article into an unstructured catalogue.
An omitted card is not an instruction to discard its documentary contribution.

**The series chooses the placement of developed qualifications.** They may
be distributed or concentrated in a limits section. Under either choice,
status and material scope travel with the claim; a late caveat never repairs
an overstated body or a misleading card.

**Status is not a stock reporting formula.** Preserve what kind of result
was obtained without repeating scientific scaffolding around every sentence.
A reported feeling is not physiological damage; self-assessed progress is
not independently checked performance. Direct wording must preserve those distinctions.

**A reassuring distortion is as false as an alarming one.** Describe what
is protected, against whom and under what conditions. A series may correct
an information imbalance; it cannot correct the evidence toward its preferred
side or require every conclusion to be unsettling.

**Report absences without upgrading them.** Not finding a measurement,
control or response does not establish that none exists. State the search
boundary where it matters and keep it in the fact file; do not turn an
access failure into a finding about the world.

**Check sourcing after writing.** New claims and stronger relationships
can enter during drafting. Verify them against the sources, not against
the writer's intended meaning. A qualification that changes the finding
changes the short form too.

### What the article does not do

- Certify its own honesty, balance or rigor. Those qualities are tested,
  not established by announcing them.
- Tell the reader what to feel or treat a desired reaction as an observed effect.
- Discuss the article itself inside a factual explanation, except where
  the subject is explicitly its sources or method.
- Substitute a moral judgment, an institution's prestige or the series'
  thesis for evidence of the particular claim.

## Design the cards before their prose

**A content card makes one coherent sourced point.** Several findings can
support it when the article supports their relationship. A study, number
or setting does not earn a card merely by existing. Covers, source cards
and navigation have their own jobs; do not invent findings for them.

Use the route map and fact file to prepare each content card:

| Preparation | Decision |
|---|---|
| Complete takeaway | What supported conclusion should remain, with its necessary scope and magnitude? |
| Reader's stake | Why would this reader care, rather than the writer or a secondary audience? |
| Plausible reply | Could they reasonably answer "true, so what?", "I finished anyway", or "I enjoy it"? |
| Evidence and scene | Which facts, actions and comparison establish the point without inventing a connection? |
| Place in the route | What does the card add to this page, and what does it prepare without leaving its own point unfinished? |

**Prepare the conclusion early; place it at the end of the proof.**
Then choose the title and stake and write the explanation that earns that
conclusion. Revise the conclusion when evidence defeats it; do not begin
with a protocol and bolt on a reason to care afterward.

**Clarity is not interest.** A reader can understand a familiar situation
and have no reason to reconsider it. Find the documented cost, constraint
or possibility that matters to them. Change the angle rather than the
evidence when the only likely reply is "so what?".

**A valued benefit and a cost can coexist.** Do not require the reader to
deny an enjoyable experience before understanding its conditions or consequences.
Identify the actual tradeoff or controllable condition. A legitimate need,
habit or pleasure is not itself the defect to correct.

### Compose the explanation

**The title states the strongest supported point, not the strongest wording.**
Keep a necessary possibility, comparison or attribution; remove empty hesitation.
The title must not promise what the proof fails to deliver. Follow the
series' title form without manufacturing a reversal.

**The stake addresses the reader; the proof establishes the point.** The
stake explains what changes for them. The proof reports actions, evidence
and results impersonally rather than advising the reader, praising the
page or merely restating its stake.

**Establish the frame before the action.** If participants are taking a
test, say so before narrating their movements. Show who does what, what
changes and what follows. Receiving a call is not holding a conversation;
changing activity is not necessarily completing an exercise.

**Narrative detail comes from evidence.** Do not invent dialogue, thoughts,
sensory cues or characters to make a study feel lived. A clearly conditional
illustration may explain a mechanism when appropriate; it must not masquerade
as an observed event or supply evidence for a claim.

**Keep the scene and comparison, not an obligatory methods abstract.**
Measurement details belong in the article unless omitting them changes
the result the reader understands. A questionnaire or arithmetic detour
must not interrupt the conclusion simply to display scientific procedure.

**Use ordinary, concrete language in every field.** Prefer active verbs and
explicit subjects. Check pronouns and clause attachment, not only spelling.
A grammatical sentence can depict the wrong event. Replace vague pointers
such as "this result" when the reader must guess what was measured or compared.

**Remove detours before removing the scene.** Length follows the explanation
and the local format, not an arbitrary quota. Several paragraphs can serve
one point. Cut stock commentary and unnecessary apparatus before the details
that make the action intelligible; reconsider structure if it will not fit.

### Make the conclusion a complete reading path

**Close the proof with its complete takeaway.** If completion, greater speed
and greater stress together form the finding, keep them together. A benefit
detached from its cost, or a qualifier detached from its result, can make
the short reading communicate the opposite of the argument.

**Use one final emphasis by default.** In content cards, reserve bold or
highlighting for that complete conclusion, as one span, rather than scattered
numbers, scene details or intermediate results. Explicit format overrides
can change the treatment, not the requirement for an accurate scanning path.

**The conclusion must survive without its lead-in.** Retain the population,
time window, comparison and magnitude needed for its meaning. A general
sentence about the topic does not replace the quantified finding that makes
the point worth knowing.

**Necessary repetition is not waste.** A reader may see only the title,
only the emphasis or the whole card. Repeat a material scope or measure
across these layers when needed. Do not remove it as redundant and leave
the most visible statement less accurate than the ordinary text.

**A separate figure must earn its place.** If the format uses one, it should
add useful information rather than restate the title as a large number.
It must not create a competing takeaway. Necessary scope repeated for an
independent reading path is a different matter.

### Keep every unit understandable on its own

**Explain the labels the unit depends on.** An unfamiliar acronym or technical
term cannot rely on an expansion elsewhere in the series. Introduce what
a tool does before its brand name. Familiarity is judged for the intended
audience, not the writer.

**A venue name validates nothing for a reader who cannot place it.**
Supply the facts that make this comparison intelligible and defensible,
not a mandatory credentials checklist. The citation identifies the actual
document, not merely a prestigious institution or an internal research report.

**Every content card gives a usable source route.** Follow the format's
citation or note convention so the reader can identify the document behind
the claim. A link to the series in general is not that route. Verify that
the target supports what is attributed to it.

**Date statements that can expire.** "Today", "now" and "no longer" become
false without an edit. Keep the relevant date or event in the statement
or its immediate context, and recheck time-sensitive facts before reuse.

## Compose the page and its place in the series

**Open with the useful question or finding, not the apparatus.** A reversal
confronts the belief; an exposition starts with what surprises or makes the
rest legible. This ordering does not forbid the brief frame needed to
understand the first scene. Do not hide the subject behind methodology.

**Keep a coherent move together when it is readable.** An opening may span
several cards, but each content card still needs a defensible point.
Splitting a scene from its comparison just to allocate one card per study
can leave the reader with two incomplete explanations.

**A source card is not automatic.** Include one when required or when it
explains how this subject is known, including file-wide limitations. Place
it after the opening. It neither replaces each claim's scope nor earns
its place by listing generic source categories.

**Let the argument determine the progression.** A useful default moves from
what the reader recognizes to the mechanism, measured extent, wider consequences
and possibilities for action. A legal prerequisite or another logical dependency
can require a different order. Do not reproduce one template across every topic.

**End on a real lever when one exists.** A right, setting, check or collective
possibility can reopen the subject; an unverifiable exhortation cannot.
Do not invent an alternative or a protection to obtain an optimistic ending.
When no lever is established, say so rather than manufacture agency.

**Agency is not necessarily a way out of immediate harm.** If a reader may
be living the harm described, name an appropriate, reachable support service
or remedy under the audience's safety rules. Verify its scope and accessibility;
a service that exists but handles another problem is not a remedy.

**Explanation must not become a guide to reproducing harm.** Avoid unnecessary
procedural detail or discovery keys that expose people or lead vulnerable
readers into harmful spaces. Preserve the evidence and understanding needed
for the point without supplying the harmful operation.

**An announced count has a defined unit and is exact.** The series decides
whether it counts points, content cards or another unit; it does not follow
from the number of studies. Apparatus and runtime slides may be counted
differently by the tool. Reconcile the conventions before correcting a mismatch.

## The re-readings

Use all five passes on a first presentation. On a return, use the affected
passes from the invalidation table and inspect the related corpus when a
shared property changed. Each pass records its actual scope:

1. **Article alone.** Read continuously after edits, including lexical ones.
   Check the argument, duplicated or stranded prose, attribution and the
   rules against self-certification and prescribed feelings.
2. **Cards and reading paths.** Read each whole card, then title-stake-conclusion,
   then the foregrounded conclusion alone. Check the plausible reader reply,
   intelligible scene, complete point, scope and magnitude. Read awkward prose aloud.
3. **Sources without the draft.** Enumerate the load-bearing evidence and look
   for what the text missed. This checks omissions, not merely whether familiar
   written sentences seem to agree with their citations.
4. **Cards against article.** Check every claim, figure, quotation, comparison
   and inference, including the scanning-only version. The short form may be
   shorter, never more certain, causal or general than its support.
5. **Page and series route.** Recheck entry, gain and next opening at each
   scale. Inspect one shared property across the relevant units at a time:
   contributions, transitions, terminology, source reuse, links and counts.

**A judgment check starts from a list, not a pattern.** Enumerate the claims,
quotations, single-study findings or units to inspect, then walk the list.
A search can locate evidence or defects; its silence does not prove that
the unsearched class is absent.

**Use another review as evidence to examine, not as work to repeat blindly.**
Check its entries against the current files and sources. Record fixed,
already resolved or rejected findings with reasons. Neither agreement with
the reviewer nor confidence in one's prose substitutes for that verification.

**A reception hypothesis is not a measured reaction.** Readability checks,
an imagined reader and the author's feedback serve different purposes.
Record what feedback actually says without declaring that every reader will
be persuaded or that a successful pilot validates the whole corpus.

## Tools, previews and handoff

**Use the tool source selected by the environment and the author.** When
the workflow uses executables from PATH, invoke those rather than downloading
an isolated copy. Record versions observed; do not freeze tools or themes
without authorization. Consult the current tool contract before guessing flags.

**Keep editorial, presentation and publication work distinct.** Do not solve
a wording problem by changing navigation, styling or published output without
approval. Build previews in the designated workspace, keeping generated files,
caches and the author's configuration within their authorized scope.

**Validate the actual artifact with the same relevant options.** Check the
rendered text, links, defaults and requested alternatives, not an older preview
or merely the input. A clean build verifies a format, not the reader's
understanding; report partial success and expected exclusions precisely.

**A copy is not always a completed delivery.** Use the designated handoff
mechanism, including registration or publication of the artifact when required.
Check before replacing an existing file, preserve authorized history, and
verify identity and availability afterward. Do not bypass the exchange workflow.

**Proposed, delivered and published are different states.** A request for
discussion, a draft or an export does not imply installation, a commit,
a tag, a push or publication. Record the state and the next decision before
handing back control; perform consequential operations only when authorized.

## What still breaks silently

**Bulk operations report successes more readily than misses.** Verify the
whole affected sequence or set, not only an operation's count. A narrowed
pattern can become silent while the defect remains; do not tune a check
merely to make its output look clean.

**Read what an edit produced.** Balanced markup can surround a broken sentence;
a removed phrase can leave two subjects or no verb. If repetitions appear,
check whether an entire section or file was duplicated before repairing
individual sentences inside the duplicate.

**References have a lifecycle and a scope.** A removed call does not remove
a note still used elsewhere. Check calls, definitions, anchors and the fact
each citation actually supports. A valid link attached to the wrong claim
is still a sourcing defect.

**Native format support is safer than unnecessary raw markup.** Custom wrappers
can bypass citation handling, typography or future format fixes. Use the
format's supported structure and verify the rendered result; syntactically
valid input is not proof that every authored element reached the reader.

**An insertion stales the pages describing the series.** Recheck inventories,
reading paths, recommendations, counts and references to positions or section
numbers. Preserve a contribution's new home and state what remains outside
the current lot instead of declaring the series globally repaired.

**Working notes use the author's working language.** Keep internal jargon
out of them when it would leak into the presentation. Write decisions,
source boundaries and unresolved points so a later session can continue
without inventing what an earlier check established.

**A correction after publication is visible and dated.** A reader who checked
yesterday must be able to identify what changed. Preserve the trace of the
correction and its evidence rather than silently replacing the claim and
calling the earlier version as though it had always said the same thing.
