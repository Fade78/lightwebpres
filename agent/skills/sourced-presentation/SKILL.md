# Sourced editorial presentation checklist

Build an editorial pair: **a public slide presentation and a referenced
long-form article**. Together they form a page, possibly within a series.
**Establish and argue in the article; make the point understandable and
memorable in the cards.** Editorial force comes from selection, relevance
and explanation, not inflated facts.

| Part | Required success |
|---|---|
| Article | An independently readable, seriously established argument, with sources, reasoning, pertinent objections and limits; not expanded slide notes. |
| Cards | Focused, compelling points worth the audience's attention; not miniature papers or unsupported slogans. |
| Connection | Every card's claims and implied relationships are established in the article. Cards may select and reorder, not strengthen the evidence. |

Local specifications set audience, language, format, tools and permissions;
they cannot authorize unsupported claims. Record checks only for units
actually examined. Reuse existing research. For a new presentation, apply
all gates and all five reading passes; for a correction, reopen affected
gates. Use this checklist as a workflow, not a prose template.

## Choose the editorial intention

| Dominant intention | Reader's question | Specific check |
|---|---|---|
| Explain or compare | How does this work; what differs? | Organize by relevant shared criteria; neither disconnected lists nor false equivalence. |
| Review knowledge | What is established, how confidently, and what remains open? | Weigh the relevant evidence and uncertainty; disclose search scope rather than imply unwarranted exhaustiveness. |
| Argue a position | Why accept this idea, interpretation or change? | State the thesis, justify the reasoning, distinguish facts from value premises, and address decisive objections fairly. |

These are working intentions, not exclusive genres. Combine them when
useful, marking the passage from findings to interpretation or recommendation.
Audience and form—exposition or belief reversal—are separate choices.
A scientific review can argue a conclusion; advocacy cannot select truth
by convenience. The intention governs both article and cards.

## Prepare

- [ ] **1 — Scope and permission.** Read the current mandate, source packet,
  article, cards and work state. Identify what changed and which units it
  affects. Discussion is not permission to edit or publish. Reuse dated
  research; reopen affected, ambiguous or time-sensitive claims. For a
  revision, consult [change triggers](references/verification.md#change-triggers).

- [ ] **2 — Audience and route.** Choose the dominant intention and name the
  reader's knowledge, question and intended gain. Map entry → gain → next
  opening for series, page and card. Each unit must work when shared alone.
  Specialists need an inspectable inference; non-specialists need explained
  terms and comparisons, not weaker evidence.

- [ ] **3 — Evidence ledger.** Before new research, define questions and
  selection criteria. For each claim, record a dated assessment, exact
  source, access level, scope and limits. A synthesis supplies leads, not
  proof. Check relevant support, counterevidence, null and inconclusive
  findings using the same criteria. See [evidence checks](references/evidence.md).

- [ ] **4 — Establish the article.** Build its own argument: facts, reasoning,
  pertinent objections and limits, including useful material beyond the
  cards. Verify claims against sources and revisit sources for omissions.
  Establish a card's new claim in the article first. Neither the editorial
  thesis nor a bibliography substitutes for evidence.

## Write

- [ ] **5 — Find the card's force.** Prepare one complete takeaway, its
  stake for the reader, a plausible objection and supporting facts. Which
  documented contrast, consequence or mechanism makes it worth attention?
  Accuracy and clarity alone are insufficient. An objection may be right.
  Several findings can serve one point; no compulsory card per study.

- [ ] **6 — Explain in reading order.** Establish the needed frame before
  actions. At each sentence, identify actor, action, object and connection
  to what precedes. Resolve pronouns; compare like objects; distinguish
  what a document says from what an actor does. Define unfamiliar terms.
  Use the [explanation test](references/explanation.md#explanation-test).

- [ ] **7 — Preserve evidential strength.** Keep attribution, population,
  conditions and material uncertainty with the claim. Do not invent facts,
  scenes, quotations, figures, motives or causal links. Distinguish allegation,
  finding, settlement and admission; observation and experiment; reported
  feeling and measured performance. Silence is not an admission. Check
  [status and quantities](references/evidence.md#status-and-quantities).

- [ ] **8 — Make every layer accurate.** The title states a supported point;
  the stake explains its relevance; the body earns the conclusion. Default
  to one final emphasized, complete takeaway. It must retain the scope,
  comparison and magnitude needed alone. Keep pertinent benefits and costs
  together; invent neither for symmetry.

- [ ] **9 — Select, do not compress mechanically.** Cards need not copy the
  article's order or methodological apparatus. Cut detours before the scene
  or material qualifications; repeat scope where independent reading requires
  it. Length serves explanation, not a quota. Each content card needs an
  identifiable source route, not just a series link.

- [ ] **10 — End on the established implication.** Make transitions explain
  relationships, not just announce the next page. Preserve each unit's
  contribution when moving it. Use a documented lever when relevant, not
  a compulsory optimistic ending. For readers facing harm, verify a suitable
  remedy and omit unnecessary details enabling abuse.

## Verify and deliver

- [ ] **11 — Read before the conclusion.** Read the explanation in order
  without borrowing clarity from the highlighted ending or your knowledge
  of the sources. Can the reader identify the events and relationships
  already described? The conclusion may add a deduction; it must not repair
  an ambiguous subject, comparison or unexplained operation.

- [ ] **12 — Test both ends.** Read the article continuously: is its argument
  established and defensible? Read each card whole, then title–stake–conclusion,
  then conclusion alone: is its point intelligible, consequential and memorable?
  What earns that impact? Check evidential scope throughout. A solid article
  does not certify compelling cards, or compelling cards a solid article.

- [ ] **13 — Verify the connection and route.** Enumerate affected claims
  and units. Trace every card's claims and implied relationships to the
  article and sources; check omissions, repetition and stranded contributions.
  Revisit the page and series progression. Would reversed outcomes receive
  the same scrutiny? A successful pilot or empty search is not exhaustive verification.

- [ ] **14 — Check the actual artifact.** Use the authorized toolchain and
  current format contract. Check fields retained or ignored, notes, links,
  anchors, counts and requested variants. When rendering is authorized,
  inspect the actual output with matching options. Otherwise state the
  untested layer; never override a no-build instruction to complete a check.

- [ ] **15 — Hand off the verified state.** Inspect the diff and recheck
  changes from reviewers rather than accepting them blindly. Follow the
  authorized delivery process; verify identity and availability, protect
  existing work and date corrections. Distinguish proposed, delivered,
  installed and published. Report remaining uncertainty; imagined reception
  is not measured reader feedback.
